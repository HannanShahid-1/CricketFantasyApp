# Turning the IPL into a Sleeper league

Almost all of season-long fantasy football carries over to the IPL. The commissioner-led league, snake draft, weekly head-to-head matchups, FAAB waivers, trades, live scoring and a playoff bracket all work for cricket with little change. Three things don't carry over and need real design work. First, IPL teams play **1 or 2 matches in a Monday-to-Sunday week, never exactly one each**. Second, the playing XI is announced only at the toss, and the Impact Player rule allows a substitute. Third, cricket roles are blurry because of all-rounders and keeper-batters. Here is the recommended design. Use **8 Monday-to-Sunday gameweeks** across the 70-match league stage: 6 regular-season weeks with a league-median game, then a 4-team playoff in weeks 7 and 8. Sum each player's points across all his matches that week. Lock each player at his own match start, with auto-subs for players left out of the XI. Keep your **4 BAT / 4 BOWL / 2 FLEX / 1 WK** lineup, but give each all-rounder eligibility at his *primary* role plus FLEX rather than a slot of his own. For data, use **Cricsheet**: free, ball-by-ball, 1,243 IPL matches, attribution licence. It is enough to build and replay entire past seasons on one PC. A paid feed (SportMonks starts at **€29/month**) is needed only for live play. On the market side, India's 2025 ban on real-money gaming means the app must stay strictly free-to-play there. That ban also emptied the field: no one offers a Sleeper-quality draft league for cricket, and Sleeper itself doesn't cover the sport.

## Fantasy football's season loop transfers almost intact

Over a season, a fantasy football manager goes through six stages, and every major platform runs them in the same order. **(1) League creation.** A commissioner creates a league, invites friends by link, and sets the configuration: league size, roster slots, scoring, draft type, waiver system, trade rules and playoff format. Some settings lock when the draft starts. ESPN, for example, freezes the waiver system then ([ESPN Waivers](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000041152-Waivers-Overview)). ESPN calls 10 teams "traditional" and recommends even team counts so nobody gets a bye ([ESPN settings guide](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/19540805)). Yahoo's default is 10 teams playing head-to-head on points ([Yahoo defaults](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN6489.html)). In a **redraft** league, which is where you're starting, every team begins each season with an empty roster. Keeper and dynasty formats carry players over from year to year ([Sleeper league types](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3537396-league-types-formats)).

**(2) The draft.** Snake is the universal default: the order reverses each round, so in a 10-team league the team with pick 1 also has pick 20. Auction is the main alternative ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/19540805)). Sleeper also offers linear drafts and a "3rd Round Reversal" that offsets the advantage of the first pick. If a manager's pick timer runs out, the system picks for them: first from their queue, then the best-ranked player who fills a roster need ([Sleeper draft types](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/1876072-what-draft-types-are-supported); [Sleeper autopick](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4038850-where-does-the-cpu-auto-pick-from)). Draft order also sets the first waiver order, with the last pick getting top priority ([Yahoo waivers](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN6427.html)).

**(3) The weekly loop.** Each week the manager sets a lineup. Most leagues lock each player at his own kickoff rather than locking the whole lineup at the week's first game ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/19540805)). Sleeper's **AutoSubs** let a manager pair a bench player with a starter. If the starter is inactive, the bench player moves in automatically, provided his game hasn't started ([Sleeper AutoSubs](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9731991-how-does-player-autosubs-work)). Once a player's game starts, he goes onto **waivers** until a scheduled processing time; Yahoo's weekly waivers clear after 11:59 p.m. PT Tuesday. Claims are settled in one of three ways. Under rolling priority, the successful claimer drops to the bottom of the order. Under reverse standings, the order resets every week. Under **FAAB**, managers place blind bids from a season budget, $100 by default, $0 bids are allowed, and ties go to waiver priority. Players nobody claims become first-come-first-served free agents ([Sleeper waiver types](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support); [Sleeper FAAB](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/1876040-how-does-faab-bidding-work); [Yahoo waivers](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN6427.html)). **Trades** follow a set sequence: one manager proposes, the other accepts, there is an optional review or league-veto window, and then the trade processes. ESPN advises using vetoes only for collusion, never for lopsided deals, and calls a trade deadline "generally a good idea" in redraft leagues ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/19540805)).

**(4) Live scoring and corrections.** A data provider streams stats during games and keeps correcting them for days afterward. Sleeper says corrections can arrive through Thursday and that the weekly report doesn't mark a matchup as final ([Sleeper stat corrections](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2441282-stat-corrections)). **(5) Standings.** Teams are ranked by record, then points for, then points against ([Sleeper tiebreakers](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4238872-can-i-set-tiebreakers)). Sleeper's optional **league-median game** gives every team a second result each week: a win if it scored in the top half of the league ([Sleeper median](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3971690-extra-game-each-week-against-league-median)). **(6) Playoffs.** These usually take 4 or 6 teams over NFL weeks 15 to 17. ESPN warns against making Week 18 the championship because real NFL starters rest that week ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/19540805)). Teams that miss the playoffs can play in a consolation bracket or Sleeper's "Toilet Bowl" ([Sleeper consolation](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2203534-consolation-bracket-vs-toilet-bowl)).

Sleeper's advantage over this standard loop is social rather than mechanical. League chat is a swipe away on nearly every screen, and trades, waivers and commissioner actions post into the chat automatically. Its CEO cited about **50 minutes a day** of in-season use, though that is the company's own figure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1868752); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/08/25/sleeper-casual-fantasy-football-start-up-battling-yahoo-and-espn.html)). The category is large: about **57 million** people play fantasy sports in the US and Canada ([FSGA](https://thefsga.org/new-fsga-research-highlights-industry-stability-and-next-generation-growth-in-fantasy-sports-and-sports-betting)), and ESPN alone had more than 13 million fantasy football players in 2024 ([ESPN Press Room](http://espnpressroom.com/press-release/another-all-time-record-13-million-fans-playing-espn-fantasy-football-in-2024/)). The table below lists the "plug and play" translation; the following sections explain the rows that need changes.

| Season stage | Fantasy football norm | IPL translation (recommended default) |
|---|---|---|
| League size | 10 teams, even count | **8 teams** default, 10 maximum (smaller player pool) |
| Season | 17 NFL weeks, playoffs weeks 15-17 | 8 fantasy gameweeks over the 70-match league stage |
| Draft | Snake, auction optional, queue plus autopick | Same; rankings seeded from last season's fantasy points |
| Lineup | QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, TE, FLEX, K, DEF + bench | 1 WK, 4 BAT, 4 BOWL, 2 FLEX + 4-5 bench |
| Lock | Per-player at kickoff | Per-player at his first match start of the week |
| Bye weeks | Occasional zero-game weeks | Every week: 1 or 2 matches per team (rarely 0 or 3) |
| Inactive starters | AutoSubs | Auto-sub when a starter isn't in the XI at the toss |
| Waivers | Tuesday-night FAAB, $100 | Monday-morning IST FAAB run, optional Thursday run |
| Trades | Review window, deadline | Same; deadline at the start of Week 5 |
| Corrections | Through Thursday | Provisional for about 48 hours after each match |
| Playoffs | 4-6 teams, avoid Week 18 | 4 teams in Weeks 7-8; skip the thin IPL playoff week |
| Social | Chat, transaction feed, push alerts | Same, plus "XI announced / not in XI" alerts |

## Eight Monday-to-Sunday gameweeks fit the 74-match IPL

The IPL has used the same shape for three straight seasons: **10 teams, 70 league matches (14 per team) and 4 playoff matches, over 65-74 days**. The 2026 season ran from 28 March to a 31 May final ([2026 IPL – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Indian_Premier_League)). Expansion to 84 or 94 matches has been promised several times and postponed each time. The IPL chairman now points to growth when the next media-rights cycle begins in 2028, with no new teams ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/sports/ipl-likely-to-grow-bigger-from-2028-no-new-teams-to-be-added-1503426097.html)). The pattern within each week is very regular. There is one evening match (19:30 IST) on every weeknight and a double-header (15:30 and 19:30) on most weekend days, with **11-12 double-headers a season**. No team has ever played on consecutive days, and the usual gap between a team's matches is 3-5 days. These figures come from parsing the full fixture lists for [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_Premier_League), [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League) and 2026.

That parsing also answers your question about how many games each team plays per week. In a Monday-to-Sunday week, **a team almost always plays 1 or 2 league matches**. In 2026 the split across all team-weeks was 31 one-match weeks, 47 two-match weeks, 5 three-match weeks and 7 zero-match weeks. In 2024 it was 41 one-match weeks, 48 two-match weeks and a single three-match week. In no week of any season did all ten teams play the same number of matches. Every team also gets a different number of two-match weeks over a season; in 2026 that ranged from 4 (KKR) to 6 (DC, LSG, RR). Week-to-week imbalance is therefore built into the IPL calendar. Fantasy football's occasional bye week is the closest equivalent, but the IPL version happens every week and is more like managing games-per-week in fantasy basketball.

Four ways of defining a gameweek were tested against the real fixture lists:

| Gameweek design | Scoring periods per season | Match balance per team | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed Mon-Sun weeks** | 8 full weeks + opening weekend (merge into Week 1) | 1-2 matches, rarely 0 or 3 | **Recommended**: fixed weekly rhythm, simple locks and waiver times |
| Blocks of 7 matches in schedule order | 10 rounds of 5-7 days | 1-2 matches (2024 had zero exceptions) | Fairest balance, but lock days move around the calendar |
| Blocks of 10 matches | 7 rounds of about 8-9 days | Mostly 2 matches (about 72%) | Too few periods for a regular season plus playoffs |
| "Every team plays twice" windows | 5-6 uneven windows (8-15 days) | Even within each window, with leftover matches | Not workable as the main format |

**Use fixed Monday-to-Sunday weeks.** People already think about cricket by the calendar week. Every gameweek ends with the busiest days, the weekend double-headers, which gives Sunday the same climax feel as an NFL Sunday. Waivers can run at the same time every week. In a 2026-like calendar, merge the two-match opening weekend into Week 1 (11 matches in total). Weeks 2-4 then have 9 matches each and Weeks 5-8 have 8 each. Run **Weeks 1-6 as the regular season**, **Week 7 as the semifinals** (top four), and **Week 8 as the final and third-place game**, which ends on the last Sunday of the league stage. Leave the IPL's own playoff week out of fantasy, for the same reason ESPN steers leagues away from NFL Week 18: only 4 franchises play 4 matches, so most rostered players would score nothing. The app can offer that week as an optional side game. With 8 teams and only 6 regular-season weeks, each team meets just 6 of its 7 rivals, so **turn the league-median game on by default**. It doubles each team's number of results and cuts down schedule luck, which matters more in a short season than in a 14-week NFL regular season ([RotoStreetJournal](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2023/08/04/fantasy-football-league-settings-what-is-league-median-and-why-your-league-should-implement-it/)). An independent check points the same way: Pitch Rival, an existing free IPL draft-league product, uses 6 regular-season gameweeks and 2 playoff gameweeks ([Pitch Rival guide](https://pitchrival.com/guide)).

**Handle the 1-vs-2 match imbalance by summing points across all of a player's matches, and treat it as a skill.** Each player's weekly score is the total from every match he plays that week. Every player card and lineup slot should show "matches this week", the cricket version of the NFL bye-week indicator. Managers who start players from double-fixture teams will score more, and picking up a two-match player for one week becomes a waiver strategy. Leagues that want less variance can switch to two alternatives in settings: "best N matches", or a per-match average. Sleeper NBA's "Lock-In" mode is a third option, where the manager chooses which single game counts ([FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2023/10/sleeper-nba-lock-in-fantasy-basketball-what-is-lock-in-mode/amp/)). Implement these as modes in the scoring step, not as structural features.

**Lineup lock** should work per player. A starter locks when his first match of the gameweek begins and stays in his slot for the rest of the week, so all his matches count. Before that, the manager can move him freely. Locking at the scheduled first ball rather than at the toss matters for cricket. Since 2023, captains have named their XI only *after* the toss ([WION](https://www.wionews.com/sports/ipl-2023-teams-to-name-playing-xi-after-toss-as-franchises-gear-up-for-upcoming-season-574714/amp)), so the gap between toss and first ball is when managers find out who is actually playing. That gap is also when the most valuable push notifications go out: "your starter is not in the XI". For managers who miss the alert, **auto-sub** the starter with the highest-ranked eligible bench player whose match hasn't started, as Sleeper's AutoSubs do. One timing point affects diaspora users: a 19:30 IST lock is 10:00 a.m. on the US East Coast in summer, so most US and UK locks fall in the working day. That is one more reason push alerts and auto-subs are essential.

**Plan for disruptions.** In 2025 the IPL was suspended from 9 to 16 May. Two Mon-Sun weeks had only 3 league matches, 4-5 teams played no matches in each of those weeks, and the final moved to 3 June ([2025 IPL – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League)). Washouts are also common late in the season: the final week of the 2024 league stage had three abandoned matches. Build gameweeks from actual fixture dates rather than a fixed calendar. Let the commissioner merge or skip a week. Score abandoned matches on whatever was recorded before play stopped.

## The 4-4-2-1 lineup works if all-rounders earn eligibility by role

Your **1 WK / 4 BAT / 4 BOWL / 2 FLEX** starting XI is a sound base. It has the same 11 players and real-cricket balance as the official game, whose 2019 rules required 1-2 keepers, 3-5 batters, 1-3 all-rounders and 3-5 bowlers ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2826953)). The two FLEX slots do the same job as fantasy football's RB/WR/TE flex: they reward drafting talent over filling positions. The open question is all-rounders. Under current Dream11-style scoring, an all-rounder who bats in the top six and bowls four overs earns points from both the batting and bowling pools, so he has the most scoring chances of any player type. Here are three ways to handle him:

| Approach | How it works | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated AR slot | Replace one FLEX with an AR-only slot | Guarantees one AR per team, but adds a sixth label and makes "who counts as AR" a constant argument |
| Full dual eligibility (BAT + BOWL + FLEX) | Any AR fits any non-WK slot | ARs become free-floating super-assets; the draft turns into "take every all-rounder", and the BAT/BOWL slots stop meaning anything |
| **Primary role + FLEX (recommended)** | Each AR is tagged batting-AR (eligible BAT + FLEX) or bowling-AR (eligible BOWL + FLEX) | ARs keep their dual scoring but still have to fit the role structure; the FLEX slots become the natural place for the best ARs |

**Recommendation: no separate all-rounder slot, and eligibility at the primary role plus FLEX.** Sleeper already stores this as a list of fantasy positions per player, the same mechanism it uses for dual-eligible NFL players ([Sleeper API](https://docs.sleeper.com/)). A batting all-rounder can fill a BAT slot or a FLEX slot, but not a BOWL slot. That caps how many all-rounders a team can usefully start and keeps them expensive in the draft without breaking the structure. Cricsheet has no player roles, so tag them yourself. Derive the tags from the previous season's data (balls bowled per match, batting position, stumpings) and then hand-check them. Fix them before the draft and let the app add eligibility mid-season if a player's role clearly changes. Before you finalise any of this, use Cricsheet data to calculate average fantasy points per role under your chosen scoring table. No published analysis of IPL fantasy points by role exists ([Kaggle dataset that could seed it](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sukhdayaldhanday/dream-11-fantasy-points-data-of-ipl-all-seasons)). If all-rounders come out far ahead, narrow their eligibility to FLEX only; if they come out behind, use a dedicated AR slot.

**Wicketkeepers** should be eligible at WK, BAT and FLEX. Most IPL keepers bat in the top order, so the WK slot is effectively a premium batter, much like the tight end in fantasy football. Keepers are scarce. Each franchise fields one in a match, so there are only about ten regular keepers in the competition. In a 10-team league every regular keeper would be a fantasy starter and the waiver wire would have none. That scarcity is a key reason to default to **8 teams**. Base WK eligibility on squad designation, not on who kept in each match. Under the Impact Player rule, a keeper-batter sometimes plays only as an impact batter while a teammate keeps. It would be unfair if that cost him his slot.

**Bench and league depth.** Franchise squads have 18-25 players, with at most 8 overseas players per squad ([iplt20.com](https://www.iplt20.com/news/112745/ipl-player-policies-declared-for-2018-season)) and 4 overseas in a playing XI ([Wisden](https://www.wisden.com/series/indian-premier-league-2023/cricket-news/explained-ipl-impact-player-the-substitution-system-that-will-debut-in-ipl-2023)). Each match uses about 12 players per side, which means roughly 120 regular players across the league. That figure is an estimate, but it sets the scale: 8 teams × 15-16 roster spots = 120-128 rostered players, while 10 teams × 15 = 150 would leave the waiver wire full of fringe players. Recommended roster: **11 starters + 4-5 bench (15-16 total)**, plus one optional IR slot for players ruled out for the season. Pitch Rival's 15-player squad with 11 starters supports this size ([Pitch Rival](https://pitchrival.com/guide)). An optional league setting of **"maximum 4 overseas in the starting XI"** brings in a familiar IPL rule and adds an interesting constraint, but leave it off by default so first-time users aren't confused.

**Impact Player.** The rule continues through 2027 ([iplt20.com Player Regulations 2025-27](https://www.iplt20.com/news/4109/ipl-governing-council-announces-tata-ipl-player-regulations-2025-27)). Each side names its XI plus a list of substitutes (sources disagree on whether there are four or five), and one substitute can replace an XI member during the match. In effect each team fields 12 players, though only 11 can bat ([ESPNcricinfo](https://africa.espn.com/cricket/story/_/id/35999996/ipl-2023-how-much-impact-impact-player-rule-have)). The fantasy rule is simple: **a player scores if he actually took part, either by starting in the XI or by coming on as the impact substitute**. A named substitute who never comes on scores zero and should trigger the auto-sub. Dream11 gives +4 for being in the announced lineup and another +4 for appearing as a substitute ([Dream11 How to Play](https://www.dream11.com/games/fantasy-cricket/how-to-play)). In a season-long league, a single "+4 for playing" bonus that covers both cases is simpler. Expect some impact substitutes to score only from batting or only from bowling. A star batter can be used purely as an impact batter; RCB did this with captain Rajat Patidar in 2025 ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cricket/rcb-make-jitesh-sharma-captain-for-sunrisers-hyderabad-match-rajat-patidar-made-impact-player-heres-why-101748007789854.html)). This slightly lowers all-rounders' value and is one more reason to test role balance with real data.

**A scoring starting point** for when you decide on scoring: Dream11's current T20 table is the only one fully confirmed from a primary source, and it serves as the industry template. Runs are +1; boundaries +4 and sixes +6; milestones at 25/50/75/100; wickets +30, with +8 for bowled or LBW; dot balls +1; maidens +12; catches +8; stumpings and direct-hit run-outs +12; and there are strike-rate and economy bands with minimum balls faced or overs bowled ([Dream11 How to Play](https://www.dream11.com/games/fantasy-cricket/how-to-play)). Under that table, a 4-0-30-2 bowling spell and a 40 off 28 balls innings each score about 80 points, so bat and ball are roughly balanced. Daily fantasy games use a captain (2x) and vice-captain (1.5x). That multiplier is optional in a season-long head-to-head league, where weekly results already vary a lot; leave it off by default.

## Cricsheet powers the prototype; a €29 feed powers launch

**Cricsheet solves the prototype's data problem completely.** It publishes an IPL-only JSON download of **1,243 matches** with full ball-by-ball detail: batter, bowler, runs, extras by type, wicket type, dismissed player, fielders involved, and substitutions (`replacements`), which is where Impact Player changes appear ([Cricsheet downloads](https://cricsheet.org/downloads/); [Cricsheet JSON format](https://cricsheet.org/format/json/)). That covers every input to a Dream11-style scoring table. Runs and boundaries come straight from the batter's run count. Balls faced are every delivery except wides. Dot balls, maidens and economy follow from runs conceded. Catches and stumpings come from the wicket type and the named fielder. One input needs an approximation: Cricsheet doesn't flag whether a run-out was a direct hit, so infer it from the number of fielders listed. Cricsheet's people register maps its player IDs to ESPNcricinfo (18,760 people) and BCCI (1,329) IDs. Its coverage of Cricbuzz IDs is tiny (50), so linking to a commercial feed later will need a custom crosswalk ([Cricsheet register](https://cricsheet.org/register/)). Most of the data is licensed **ODC-BY**: commercial use is allowed with attribution ([Cricsheet licensing](https://cricsheet.org/licensing/)). There are three limits. Cricsheet is **not live**, since matches appear after they are played (about a day later in practice). It has **no player roles**. And it has **no ball-by-ball timestamps or exact start times**, so you will have to generate those for replay.

Live play needs a paid feed. No vendor publishes a measured latency guarantee, so plan on polling once on the server and pushing updates to all users, never polling from each user's device.

| Source | Role | Price (list) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| CricketData.org | Testing a live adapter | Free 100 hits/day; $5.99/mo | Cheaper, slower, less depth ([freeapihub](https://freeapihub.com/apis/cricketdata-api)) |

**Don't scrape ESPNcricinfo, Cricbuzz or iplt20.com.** Disney, ESPN's parent company, has terms elsewhere that prohibit automated scraping ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/disney/disney-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-022520/prohibition-on-automated-access-and-ai-scraping/)), and unofficial Cricbuzz access "can be restricted or cut off" ([Apify](https://apify.com/superapis/cricbuzz)). Scrapers also break whenever a site's layout changes. Licensed factual data is the safer foundation. Before launch, read each vendor's terms on fantasy use and redistribution, because none of the pricing pages covers them.

## India's 2025 money-gaming ban makes free-to-play the only path

**The legal ground shifted under Indian fantasy in August 2025.** The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans online money games, explicitly including paid fantasy, whether or not they involve skill. The Act and its Rules came into force on **1 May 2026** ([Supreme Court Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/cases/challenge-to-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025/)). A money game is defined broadly: any game where a user pays fees, deposits or "other stakes" expecting a monetary return. Industry bodies say "other stakes" could cover purchased coins or tokens ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/gaming-news/meity-consults-gaming-industry-stakeholders-raise-red-flags-over-stakes-definition-82458.htm)). Offering a money game can bring up to 3 years in prison and/or a ₹1 crore fine ([LatestLY](https://www.latestly.com/gaming/dream11-pauses-paid-contests-mpl-cuts-60-of-india-staff-five-months-under-the-new-online-gaming-rules-2-7632746.html)). "Online social games" remain permitted. A Supreme Court challenge is pending with no interim relief. A separate June 2026 Supreme Court ruling held that staking money on an uncertain outcome is betting regardless of skill ([TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=16658)), which makes it unlikely paid fantasy returns soon. **No official guidance specifically confirms that free fantasy is permitted.** Still, an app with no entry fees, no deposits, no purchasable currency that affects results and no cash prizes fits the definition of a social game. Two consequences follow for design. Sleeper's **Dues Tracker**, which only records off-platform payments ([Sleeper](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/11862228-fantasy-league-dues-tracker)), should not be built for Indian users at all. And revenue must come from ads, sponsorships, and paid cosmetic or commissioner-tool upgrades, similar to Yahoo's "Commissioner Plus" ([Yahoo](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN36403.html)). Outside India, free-to-play fantasy carries little risk; paid entry would bring in US state-level fantasy rules and UK pool-betting licensing ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/community/insights/legal/practical-guidance-journal/b/pa/posts/10-legal-issues-with-fantasy-sports); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/14653/uk-gaming-commission-fantasy-sports/)).

**The ban has also cleared the competitive field.** Dream11 stopped paid contests on 22 August 2025 and said about 95% of group revenue disappeared ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/dream11-rmg-ban-new-strategy-revenue-loss-layoffs-125082600219_1.html)). MPL cut about 60% of its India staff ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/21/as-india-bans-real-money-games-dream-sports-mpl-start-pulling-the-plug)). The official IPL Fantasy game for 2026 is free and hosted by My11Circle. It is a salary-cap game with transfers, in which everyone can pick the same stars, not a draft league ([CricketWinner](https://www.cricketwinner.com/cricket-news/ipl-introduces-official-fantasy-league-ahead-of-2026-season/)). Draft-league products for cricket do exist. CricBattle has run private leagues since 2011 and claims 1.5 million IPL users ([CricBattle](https://www.cricbattle.com/)), and newer entrants include Pitch Rival and CricFantasy, which advertises "NFL-style snake & auction drafts" ([CricFantasy](https://cricfantasy.app/)). None appears to have Sleeper-level polish or scale. Sleeper itself covers the NFL, NBA, soccer and others but not cricket ([Sleeper App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sleeper-fantasy-sports/id987367543)). CricBattle's 15 years at modest scale suggest the demand is real but niche, and that growth will come from distribution through friend groups, WhatsApp chats and office leagues more than from features. The draft format's real selling point is **exclusivity**: if you drafted Bumrah, nobody else in your league has him. That creates the rivalries that keep a friends' league talking all season. Diaspora fans in the US, UK and Canada are the segment most likely to pay for a premium subscription, because they already know Sleeper and ESPN football leagues. GullyCricket's US-focused positioning shows these users can be reached ([GullyCricket](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gullycricket-fantasy-cricket/id1490300794)).

**Branding.** Player names and match statistics are generally free to use in India. In 2023 the Delhi High Court refused to block a fantasy product's use of cricketers' names and stats, though that was an interim ruling ([Columbia Global Freedom of Expression](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/cases/digital-collectibles-v-galactus/)). IPL and franchise logos, official photographs and anything implying official status require a licence. Keep "IPL" out of the app's name, use it only to describe what the app covers, and use generic player avatars.

## A virtual clock lets one PC replay a whole season

The prototype's central idea is that **the app never reads the real clock**. A simulation clock tracks a virtual "now" that you can run at 1x, 60x, 600x or "instant". Lineup locks, waiver runs, draft pick timers and end-of-week scoring all run off that clock. A replay engine takes a past IPL season from Cricsheet, places each match at its real date and its usual 15:30 or 19:30 IST start, and releases deliveries one at a time with generated timing. A reasonable spacing is 35-45 seconds per ball, longer gaps after wickets and boundaries, and a 10-20 minute innings break. At 60x speed a T20 match plays out in about 3.5 minutes. The scoring engine processes each ball as it arrives, updates live matchup scores, and sends changes to the screen. If the replay feed produces the same data shape as the eventual paid API, switching to live data later only means swapping the data source. Bot managers with fixed random seeds fill the other league slots: they draft from rankings with some noise, set lineups by projected points and the week's fixture count, and bid FAAB on recent form. Seeded bots make every season reproducible, so a code change that alters final standings shows up as a failed test.

Keep the architecture simple. Use **PostgreSQL from the start, even locally**, because draft picks and waiver runs depend on row locking and job queues that SQLite doesn't support. A Postgres-backed job queue can handle locks, waivers and pick timers without adding another service ([pg-boss](https://npmjs.com/package/pg-boss)). Use WebSockets for the draft room, where data flows both ways, and the same connection or Server-Sent Events for live scores ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Server-sent_events/Using_server-sent_events)). Copy Sleeper's public data model for leagues, rosters, matchups, transactions and drafts; it has been proven at scale ([Sleeper API](https://docs.sleeper.com/)). A TypeScript web app plus a small Python data-ingestion script is a sensible pairing. An all-Python stack also works if that's what you know. On Windows 11, develop inside WSL2 with the code stored on the Linux filesystem, which Docker's documentation says performs much better and keeps hot reload working ([Docker Docs](https://docs.docker.com/desktop/features/wsl/best-practices/)). The pieces you will have to build from scratch are the draft room, the FAAB processor, the cricket scoring engine and the replay simulator. No open-source season-long cricket league manager exists to start from.

| Phase | What gets built | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Data | Load Cricsheet IPL data; per-match player stat lines; role tags; scoring engine checked by hand against 3-5 known matches | Points are correct, and you get role-average data for the AR/WK decision |
| 1. League and clock | League settings, rosters, gameweek generator, virtual clock, replay feed, round-robin schedule, standings | An all-bot league plays a full season in seconds |
| 2. Draft room | Snake draft, pick timer, queue and autopick, bot drafters, draft board | Real-time drafting with you plus nine bots |
| 3. In-season loop | Lineup screen with eligibility and locks, auto-subs, live matchup ticker, FAAB waivers, free agency | A full simulated season played week by week |
| 4. Trades and polish | Trades with review, commissioner tools, playoff bracket, chat, notifications | The complete Sleeper-style loop |
| 5. Productionise | Real login, hosted database, live data feed (SportMonks), mobile app | Ready to publish |

**Use the simulator as a design lab, not only as a test harness.** Before writing any user-interface polish, replay **2024** (the cleanest season: every team played once or twice in every 7-match block) and then **2025** (a suspension, washouts and a replayed match) under different rule sets. That answers the open questions with evidence. How far ahead do all-rounders finish under the primary-role-plus-FLEX rule? Does summing a two-match week decide too many matchups compared with best-of-N scoring? Do 8 teams leave a usable waiver wire, and do 10 teams leave none? How often does the league median change who makes the playoffs? Each answer is one setting change and one replay.

## Conclusion

The fantasy-football side of this app is a solved problem. Sleeper, ESPN and Yahoo have already settled the lifecycle, the waiver rules and the data model. What makes cricket different is a short list of mechanics: uneven fixture counts each week, playing XIs confirmed only at the toss with an impact substitute on top, and all-rounders who score in two pools at once. Treat each as a design feature. A "matches this week" counter and a player locked in for all his matches turn fixture imbalance into strategy. The window between toss and first ball becomes the moment for alerts and auto-subs. Primary-role eligibility makes the best all-rounders the players everyone wants in the draft without letting them break the roster structure. The question your design has to answer is less "how do we copy Sleeper" than "which of these three cricket mechanics do we turn into fun".

The timing favours you. The money-gaming ban removed paid fantasy's revenue model, pushed Indian users toward free play, and left the friends-league social experience, Sleeper's actual strength, without a strong cricket product. Because Cricsheet is free and complete, the riskiest rule decisions (league size, all-rounder balance, the multi-match rule) can be settled on one PC by replaying real seasons before you spend anything on a data feed or a launch. Make that replay-and-measure loop the first milestone, before any user-facing feature.
